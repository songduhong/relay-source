“They told me about the problem before it became a shipment problem.”

We came to SourcingRelay with a 47-SKU launch and a date we had already announced. What mattered most was not another polished quote. It was knowing which supplier was late, which version had changed and what would actually leave Shenzhen.

One charger line arrived with a different cable than the approved sample. The team held it, showed us the difference and proposed a replacement. The other 46 SKUs moved into consolidation while we made the decision.

The quote was not the cheapest we received. It was, however, the easiest to understand: goods, service, freight and likely duties were separated, and the assumptions were written down.

What was not perfect

Onboarding took one round longer than expected because our product photos were incomplete. The team asked for clearer model labels before quoting instead of guessing.

How reviews are collected

A review is only useful when you can judge its context.

We collect feedback after delivery, verify it against the order record, ask what went wrong and publish the customer-approved version with its limitations.

01Invite

Ask the order contact for feedback after delivery and again after 30 days.

02Verify

Match the review to the order, destination, SKU count and known exceptions.

03Challenge

Ask what was slower, unclear or frustrating — not only what went well.

04Approve

Let the customer approve the public version and redact private commercial details.

Publication rules

What we will never hide

  • A held, replaced or excluded SKU
  • A missed internal target
  • A material change in quotation assumptions
  • Whether a review or video was incentivized
  • Whether a customer agreed to be identified
Privacy still matters.

We remove personal contact details, private pricing and supplier identities when publication would break confidentiality.

A useful comparison

What makes a sourcing review trustworthy?

Low-signal review
  • Anonymous name or initials only
  • “Perfect service” with no order context
  • Five stars and no mention of friction
  • Marketing-approved wording with no consent record
  • Claims that cannot be traced to a shipment
Useful review
  • Named buyer, role and market
  • Order scope, timeline and verified outcome
  • At least one question about what could improve
  • Customer-approved publication and clear disclosure
  • Correction path shown when a SKU fails
Customer review FAQ

Questions a serious buyer should ask

Are the reviews on this page real customer feedback?

Not yet. The current examples are clearly labeled sample content for design review. Before launch, we will replace them with verified feedback from customers who have approved publication.

Why do you publish problems as well as praise?

A perfect review tells a buyer very little. Describing the issue, correction path and final outcome shows how the team behaves when the order does not go exactly to plan.

What personal data is removed?

We remove private contact information, sensitive pricing, supplier identities and anything the customer does not consent to publish. Product category, destination and order context can remain when approved.

Can I verify a review through a live order?

The Track order demo shows the type of status, SKU and QC information attached to an order. Live customer records remain private and require client access.

Can I speak to a reference customer?

For qualified buyers, we can request a reference call with a customer in a similar category. The customer decides whether and how to participate.

Do you edit customer wording?

We may correct spelling, translate into clear English and redact private data. We do not change what the customer meant or remove a disclosed problem from the final story.
