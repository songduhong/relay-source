Most accessory sellers lose money in the same place: they fall in love with a product photo, order 500 units, and discover the problems customers would have found in week one. A sample sprint fixes that — if you test the right things in the right order.

Why sample sprints fail

A typical failed sprint looks like this: 20 cases arrive, they all look great in photos, and the seller picks the cheapest one. Nobody checked whether the MagSafe magnet actually holds an iPhone 15 Pro on a car mount, whether the buttons click, or whether the camera cutout blocks the lens guard. The winner is chosen on price, not on proof.

The 48-hour test plan

Hours 0–4: intake and count

Every sample is logged with supplier, price and quantity. One SKU that arrives short or wrong is already a signal about the supplier — before any testing starts.

Hours 4–24: fit and function

  • Fit check on a real device, not a jig: corner grip, button travel, port access.
  • Camera cutout against lens guards and the widest third-party lens you sell.
  • MagSafe pull test: detach force measured, not eyeballed. Anything under the vendor claim gets flagged.
  • Print durability: tape test and 200-screen-rub on painted or IMD finishes.

Hours 24–48: scoring and correction path

Each SKU leaves the bench with a pass, a fixable flag or a fail. Fixable flags go back to the supplier with photos; fails are replaced from a second source before you ever see a bulk quote.

Key takeaway: test against your customer’s real usage — car mounts, lens guards, daily pocket wear — not the supplier’s demo video. A 20-SKU sprint costs a fraction of one bad 500-unit order.

What it costs

With 5–20 units per SKU, a 20-case sprint typically lands between $180 and $320 including local freight and QC time. Compare that with one returned-ASIN listing problem and the math stops being close.
